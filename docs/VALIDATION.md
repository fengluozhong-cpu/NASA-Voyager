# Scientific validation

Input source is the supplied NASA/JPL Horizons archive. Its 14 CSVs contain 107,502 records and have matching SHA-256 checksums, dimensions, TDB sample intervals and correct spacecraft IDs. The 12 supplemental real Horizons datasets bring the published binary cache to 194,562 records. All binary hashes, row lengths, endpoints, monotonic timestamps and finite values are checked by `npm test`. The automatically generated [manifest](../public/data/manifest.json) records exact coverage, queries, response headers, source hashes, coordinate frame and flagged velocity changes.

- **Spacecraft coverage.** Voyager 1 daily samples begin 1977-09-06 00:00 TDB, following the archive's clipped start on the daily grid. The early kernel can predate launch and is approximate. Voyager 2's first complete TDB hour after available source coverage is 1977-08-20 16:00 TDB. No vector is created before the returned epochs. The Horizons object note lists a September 6 launch, while NASA's mission record dates the launch to September 5, 1977 at 12:56:01 UT. The narrative follows the NASA mission record.
- **Time and units.** J2000 ecliptic, ICRF, Sun-centered geometric vectors and km, km/s. The archive's Voyager heliocentric and independent Earth-centered queries have aligned TDB epochs. Sun and Earth ranges are derived from vectors. The max archive Earth-range cross-check residual is 4.3271 km. Supplemental Earth positions come from DE441, while the Voyager fitted solution uses DE440 as documented in the source notes. A residual is recorded explicitly rather than assuming exact agreement between requests.
- **Flybys.** The narrative uses the NASA Voyager timeline dates for Jupiter (V1 1979-03-05), Saturn (V1 1980-11-12, V2 1981-08-25), Uranus (V2 1986-01-24) and Neptune (V2 1989-08-25), as well as Pale Blue Dot (1990-02-14) and heliopause crossings (V1 2012-08-25, V2 2018-11-05). Chapter anchors select available times on those dates and are approximate scene anchors. [NASA Voyager timeline](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/timeline/)
- **Continuity.** Rapid changes in velocity cluster in flyby windows, including Voyager 2's July 1979 Jupiter encounter and the hour around Neptune. These are retained from the supplied mission-design trajectories. The earliest V2 segment agrees with the archive at the boundary within 0.01 km. The source notes describe trajectory stitching, constraints and tracking coverage.
- **One light-day.** Root of geometric range / c = 86,400 s, bracketed by fresh hourly heliocentric Earth and Voyager 1 vectors. Approximate result is 2026-11-18 10:16 UTC. NASA independently announced Nov. 18 as the milestone date. Our exact hour is an interpolation from the model. [NASA announcement](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-invites-media-public-to-join-voyager-1-one-light-day-activities/). Earth station location, relativity and actual transmission delay are outside the calculation.
- **Limits.** Early trajectories are approximate stitched conics. Later model-based positions include long-term predictions. Earth is sampled hourly through the endpoint extension and daily elsewhere. Outer planets are sampled every five days. Linear interpolation and textures are suited to public visualization. The heliopause is a spherical diagram, while the physical boundary is dynamic and directional.
